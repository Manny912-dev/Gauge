Monitor Specs Lighting Settings
01

Monitor

Ctrl1

Live readings, ninety seconds deep.

CPU and GPU load drawn edge to edge, with temperature, clock, power and fans beside them. Memory, network, storage and every running app, on one page.

  • CPU and GPU, in fullLoad with 90 seconds of history, plus temperature, clock, power, fan and video memory where the hardware reports them.
  • Heat you can see Temperatures turn amber, then red, as they climb.
  • Memory, network, storageAt a glance, with download and upload charted live.
  • Apps, not just processesGrouped by app and sortable by CPU, GPU or memory. Run as administrator for per-app download and upload speeds.
  • More than one GPUSwitch between graphics cards on machines that have several.
  • Always lightweightAll of it in about 40 MB, so the monitor never becomes the thing you're monitoring.

Specs

Your whole machine, one copy away.

A complete hardware inventory, detected automatically. No digging through Device Manager, no third tool to install.

  • Everything, groupedSystem, processor, graphics, memory modules, storage, display and network, with the key figures in one row at the top.
  • Copy specsPuts it all on the clipboard as plain text, ready for a forum post, a support ticket or a "will this run it?" thread.

03

Lighting

Ctrl3

Your RGB, without the maker's software.

One device list for everything that lights up, with a live preview that glows in the colours you pick. Delete the vendor suites; Gauge talks to the hardware itself.

Keyboards Mice Headsets Graphics cards Coolers & fans LED controllers Motherboards & RAM

Native drivers for Lenovo Legion, LOQ and IdeaPad Gaming 4-zone keyboards and the Keychron Q1 HE. Hundreds more through the built-in OpenRGB engine.

  • Found automaticallyDevices appear on their own, through native drivers or the OpenRGB engine. No setup, no profiles to import.
  • Zones, effects, speedPer-zone colours and each device's own effects, brightness and speed, with a live preview that glows as you go.
  • Motherboard and RAMOne administrator approval installs the PawnIO driver, and there's a clean way to turn it off again.
  • Set it once"Apply when Gauge starts" puts your lighting back at every sign-in.
  • Your device, nextDevices without a driver yet are listed, with a report you can copy and send so support can be added.
  • Keychron Q1 HE NewAll 22 effects, colour, brightness and speed through VIA, saved on the keyboard itself.
04

Settings

Ctrl4

Starts quietly. Stays out of the way.

Gauge can open straight to the tray at sign-in, keep running when you close it, and update itself without asking for your attention.

Accent colourTry it here: this page uses the same eight
Custom colour#FFA53C

Looks good.

  • Start with WindowsOpens in the notification area when you sign in, without the window, so your lighting is applied every time.
  • Keep running in the trayThe close button hides Gauge instead of quitting. Click the tray icon to bring it back.
  • Quiet updatesNew versions download in the background and install the next time Gauge starts. You can switch it off.
  • Eight accents, or any hueCharts, highlights and switches follow the colour you choose.
  • Nothing extra runningA custom-drawn interface: no web view, no background services.
  • Keyboard friendlyCtrl + 1–4 moves between pages.

Privacy

Nothing leaves
your PC.

Gauge has no account and sends nothing about you or your machine anywhere. The only time it goes online is to ask GitHub whether there's a newer version of itself, and that's a switch in Settings.

Under the hood

Drawn by hand,
pixel by pixel.

Nothing on screen is a standard control. Every page is drawn directly with GDI+, which is why Gauge opens instantly and sits at around 40 MB while it works.

Built with
Interface
Custom GDI+ renderer, no web view
Runtime
C# on .NET 10, Windows Forms
Sensors
LibreHardwareMonitor, NVML, performance counters
Lighting
Native drivers and the OpenRGB 1.0 engine
Updates
Velopack, in the background

Build it yourself
PowerShell
>git clone https://github.com/Manny912-dev/Gauge.git
>cd Gauge
>dotnet build -c Release

You need Windows and the .NET 10 SDK. Read the code, open an issue, or send a pull request on GitHub.
